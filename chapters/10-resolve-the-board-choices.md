# Resolve the Board's Choices Together

Three things need a decision by Friday: the equipment, a distribution to owners and how to handle a possible cash shortage. The board could vote on each separately and approve a combination that no one can carry out. Your work is to put the choices on one dated cash sheet and write the condition under which you would reopen them.

This is a new scenario using the two original equipment alternatives. Read the assumptions as a packet for this decision, not as the year's actual performance. The numbers are fictional.

## Board packet

- Unrestricted cash before purchase or payout: $330,000. The board protects $90,000 immediately and at the end of each week. Assume no other cash restrictions solely for the case.
- Compact unit: $160,000 today, $65,000 operating cash in each of years 1–3, plus $10,000 net sale cash in year 3. Discount at 10%.
- Whole bay: $240,000 today, $100,000 operating cash in each of years 1–3, plus $30,000 net sale cash in year 3. Discount at 14% to reflect the stated higher-risk case assumption.
- The treasury team projects net firm operating cash of −$25,000 in week one and +$45,000 in week two. These liquidity entries are common to both alternatives. They are not extra incremental flows to add to either annual equipment schedule. No facility is currently committed.
- There is a separate request for a $20,000 payment to owners today. The proposed owner payment is not an additional project operating cost.
- A customer could move $30,000 from week two to week one. Do not assume the change until evidence confirms the settlement. For sensitivity, consider the customer asking for a $4,000 immediate discount to make the change; treat that as a discretionary incremental cost of choosing whole bay in the test.

## First pass, without an assistant

Open two work sheets rather than one score. On the first, show the relative NPVs at the stated rates. On the second, match each purchase with the week-zero, week-one and week-two balances, first without a payout and then with it. Add the customer timing change only as a separate scenario. List any action you refuse to recommend and the exact dated measurement that makes it fail.

Offer the board a recommendation no longer than one paragraph, plus the visible workings. Draw a box around your least certain material input and the source you would check. Name the person who can authorize the next step and the date they must decide.

## Review notes to read after making the first recommendation

Both NPVs are positive. Whole bay leads by about $3,254 in value before any discretionary acceleration cost. Its cash today is $90,000 without a payout, exactly on the limit, but without the earlier customer settlement it has only $65,000 after week one. That fails the weekly rule. Compact without payout ends week one at $145,000, so it passes the stated test. Compact with the proposed $20,000 payout also clears the two weeks supplied; the case does not tell you about the forecast beyond them.

With a confirmed fee-free acceleration and no payout, whole bay leaves $95,000 after week one; week two closes at $110,000 whether or not the receipt was accelerated. Approving the payout too would put whole bay at $70,000 immediately even if the earlier week-one settlement arrives. When the customer demands a $4,000 fee today, the project misses the day-zero floor without a further change and the cost exceeds the modeled NPV advantage. The same business term is relevant to two separate tests; record its date and amount once in each properly scoped analysis.

A limited recommendation supported by the packet: select the compact unit with no funding change unless the customer acceleration is cleared without that fee and the board defers the payout. Before recommending the payout, request a complete liquidity horizon and check existing constraints. The book has not proved that retaining all available cash indefinitely is right. The immediate action and longer-run cash policy are different decisions.

## Defend it under a new constraint

For the unaided defense, take one changed fact you did not write: the customer needs a fee paid at the end of week two, the reserve rises, or the cash ledger includes an incoming deposit restricted for a different purpose. Identify which inputs change, recompute the affected test, and explain why the other test did or did not change.

If you use an assistant after locking your answer, ask it to identify one date, unit or double-counting error. Require a reference to the exact line in the packet. Do not accept unsupported facts about financing availability, lender consent or whether a payout is allowed under actual law. Retain the original recommendation, the challenge and your reason for changing or retaining it.

Close with a decision record someone can use when the numbers change: **action now, governing constraint, next evidence, decision owner, and date to reopen**.
