# Value the Financing Separately

The sponsor lowers WACC because the new debt appears cheap. The treasurer adds the interest tax benefit as a separate line. Each method can be useful. Together, without reconciling their assumptions, they can pay the firm twice for the same deduction. Take the asset price apart before signing the financing note.

You will build an adjusted present value (APV) for a project with fixed debt, and identify which claims depend on the debt schedule. The figures are synthetic, in thousands of dollars. Rates, taxes and scenarios are stipulated for instruction rather than inferred from a live market.

## Price the project without financing first

The project requires 300 today, then supplies 100 of after-tax operating cash at the end of each of the next four years. That cash flow assumes no claims of lenders and does not assume any financing tax benefits. The example ends at year four; there is no sale or residual cash. Price the stream at 11%, the required operating return adopted for this exercise.

The PV of the receipts is about 310.245, leaving an unlevered project NPV of 10.245. This is a reference point. It tells you what has to be improved by financing without pretending that the actual company can fund the project in full with equity. It also gives you a stable comparison if the debt profile changes.

## Price each departure from that reference point

Assume a loan supplies 120 at time zero, charges 7% interest on the unchanged principal, and repays principal at the end of year four. At the stipulated matching debt yield and absent any issue cost, the net PV of these contractual lender flows is zero. The company still needs the dollars on their dates. Financing capacity and economic value are different tests.

In this case $8.40 of annual interest reduces incremental cash taxes by $2.10 each year under stipulated rules. The entire shield can be used when interest is paid. Discount those shield cash flows at 7%, which the example assumes fits their risk; that is not a universal choice for uncertain shields. Their PV is about 7.113. The company separately pays 3 today to arrange the debt. This version assumes those fees do not change the promised lender flows or the stated cash taxes.

The modeled borrowing also adds a risk of disrupted supplier arrangements at refinancing. Use a decision weight of 20% on an incremental cost of 55 in year four, with no cost in the other state. This is a cost of the borrowing scenario beyond the asset cash already priced, not an extra charge for all ordinary downside project outcomes. For instruction, discount the 11 expected cost at the specified 11%. In a live APV, the cash effect, state dependence and discounting for distress would need independent evidence.

| Adjustment | Present value ($000) | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| All-equity project NPV | 10.245 | Cash excludes financing tax shields |
| Present value of usable interest shields | +7.113 | Separate debt schedule |
| Incremental distress risk | −7.246 | Stipulated year-four expected cost |
| Issue cost | −3.000 | Cash today |
| **Adjusted present value** | **7.112** | Rounded after summing |

The APV is positive relative to doing nothing under the assumptions, but the modeled financing adjustments together detract from the unlevered NPV. If an unlevered project is truly feasible, the numbers as shown do not make this borrowing the best route. If it is not feasible, compare the available alternatives and their own funding plans.

## Test what changed

A blended rate that already imputes the interest tax benefit and an APV that explicitly adds that benefit answer different versions of the same valuation problem. Do not use the first to value the project and then add the second's shield line on top. A textbook rate with market weights can be a useful simplification for stable policies; falling or bullet debt can make a schedule more transparent. See [Stewart Myers's original capital budgeting paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1974.tb00021.x) on separating financing effects in valuation. The case does not import that paper's examples or assert that the debt principal is the only meaningful contract term.

**Before consulting a worked answer**, test the assumption that the company can use every future deduction. If the last two annual shields never produce cash savings and nothing else changes, compute the new APV. Then say why you should not replace 25% with an unsupported “average realized tax rate” if you actually have a dated tax model. Keep the revised finance effect separate from operating cash.

**Inspect the answer.** A prepared note says, “Once debt is raised, we should subtract principal at maturity from APV, even though we didn't include the initial receipt.” The principal payment belongs in the debt cash timeline. For APV the loan flows need consistent treatment on both sides. Omission of one direction does not price risk or recognize a legitimate financing cost.

**Transfer.** A lender offers an interest-only extension. The principal payment would move a year beyond the operating forecast, and the borrower would owe an additional annual interest payment. List the two analyses this triggers before committing: value of the revised financing effects under updated risks and timing, and ability to make the final contractual payment after operating cash stops. Do not treat a later date as evidence there is a refinancing market.
