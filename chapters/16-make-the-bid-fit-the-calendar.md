# Make the Bid Fit the Calendar

A seller offers to reduce the payment at closing in exchange for a future earnout. The acquisitions team describes the change as a price reduction, then tells treasury the deal no longer needs funding review. The real shift is in who pays, when, and after which event. Work the price path and the cash path independently.

You will value two proposed payment structures on an agreed illustrative basis and test the cash needed in the state that triggers the contingent payment. The buyer is fictional. This exercise deals with a modeled price, not accounting or legal treatment of a real earnout.

## Separate the thing bought from how it is paid for

The estimated value to the buyer of the acquired operations, already adjusted for integration and relevant other claims, is 446 thousand dollars today. Assume that number is independent of the proposed seller payment terms for this comparison; the seller does not keep an operational role. Consider:

1. A single payment of 430 today.
2. A payment of 400 today plus 90 at the end of year two if a specified performance event is independently certified.

A case analyst assigns a valuation weight of 50% to the payment event and uses a 12% rate for the contingent payment. Assume that convention is internally stipulated for these risks. Do not infer a live probability or market discount rate. In both offers the company has 520 of unrestricted cash just before closing and must retain 110 immediately afterward. It has no currently committed outside funding. No fees, other payments or cash rules apply today beyond those stated.

**Attempt first.** Compute the present cost to the buyer of the cash price in each option. Subtract that from the buyer's value for the asset. Then write two additional columns: cash immediately after closing, and the highest extra seller payment after closing. Do not substitute the weighted valuation input for the actual payment state.

| Bid | Present cost of price ($000) | Value to buyer net of price ($000) | Cash immediately after closing ($000) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Single payment | 430.00 | 16.00 | 90 |
| Contingent payment | 435.87 | 10.13 | 120 |

The contingent bid is more expensive on the specified valuation basis. Its later weighted payment is 45 in year two, worth about 35.87 today. It passes the closing cash rule where the single payment fails. That result does not make it cheaper or remove the 90 due if the event happens.

## Test the performance state, not an averaged successor

In the state requiring payment, treasury expects 155 of unrestricted cash immediately before paying the seller. If the 110 floor also applies then, 155 − 90 = 65 fails by 45. In the no-payment state no equivalent amount is due. A report using a 45 averaged payment in every state would leave 110 in the modeled trigger state and mistakenly claim the company clears the test. An average is an input to valuation; it is not the cash receipt or outflow in a specific state.

The contract is part of the risk, not an annotation under it. What is measured? Who can influence the metric? Who independently measures it? Is a change in distribution strategy recognized equally during the earning period? When is a disputed result finally payable? A buyer who can make the condition fail by changing a record or withholding support from the business may face a different price and conflict risk. Do not write a numerical discount for those issues without checking their mechanics.

A review recommendation could reject both bids as currently fundable even though both show positive buyer value under the stipulated inputs. The whole-price bid fails at closing. The contingent option needs a viable payment plan if the trigger occurs. Discuss an actual committed solution or renegotiate exact payment dates and terms. Do not call an unapproved assumption “seller financing” and sign.

**Inspect the answer.** A simulated memo claims the first offer “pays 430 total” while the second “pays only 435.87 total.” The second amount is a modeled present cost, not a total nominal payment. The contingent package can pay 400 or 490 in nominal dollars. Explain which quantity answers the price comparison and which quantities treasury needs.

**Transfer.** Holding the initial 400 payment, the timing, the weight and the rate fixed, compute the maximum trigger payment that matches the present price of 430. Compare that limit with the maximum actual payment supportable by the 155 cash balance and 110 floor. Which bound is stricter? State a term, not an adjusted discount rate, that could make agreement possible.
