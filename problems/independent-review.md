# Independent Review Problems

These problems use new fictional data. Solve them without a model first. For each decide **what gets measured**, **on which date**, **under which convention**, and **whether the evidence supports taking the action**. Attach your first attempt. Optional critique may challenge one line of your reasoning, but do not ask it to replace the calculation, choose the action, or supply company facts. Check any critique against the supplied inputs and retain the reason for any change.

## 1. The cash floor is not the discount rate

A repair service can add a specialized mobile unit for 125 thousand dollars today. After-tax incremental cash is 46, 49 and 53 at the ends of years one through three. At the last date the unit also produces 12 of net sale cash. The case's operating discount rate is 8%. The firm has 190 of unrestricted cash today and a floor of 60 immediately and at the end of each of two weeks. Ordinary firm cash movements outside the project purchase are −12 in week one and +18 in week two. Those movements are not incremental to the project.

A customer can bring 9 of week-two receipts into week one. The arrangement requires a fee of 1 today, attributable to purchasing the vehicle in this proposal, with no other modeled economic timing effect. The customer has not committed.

1. Compute the operating NPV and compare it with the NPV after the fee. State which present-valued flows belong to that calculation.
2. Show every date with and without acceleration and explain which cash measurements matter before ordering.
3. Replace the fee of 1 by 3, keeping every other number fixed. Does the action still pass? Determine the maximum fee consistent with the dated cash requirements in the stated accelerated scenario.
4. State which document and decision owner are missing. Don't describe a nonbinding offer as cash.

## 2. A secured loan's cash and EBITDA do not define each other

A separate company has gross qualifying debt of 182, eligible cash of 22, and cash of 35 held for an escrow purpose excluded from the defined contract measure. Contract earnings are 50 and the net debt-to-earnings test passes at or below 3.4. The lender cash payment of 9 falls on Monday, when the company expects 27 in unrestricted cash immediately before payout. A separate operating floor of 20 applies immediately after payout. These are different test dates; do not use Monday's 27 to change the specified ratio numerator.

1. Reconcile the contract and cash tests. Calculate contractual debt headroom and separately calculate the Monday shortfall.
2. A manager proposes using 8 of existing eligible cash at the covenant date to repay debt and says that improves the multiple. Show exactly which balance lines change. Then explain why you must recalculate any effect on the separate Monday cash forecast using actual payment timing.
3. Suppose a new contractual definition explicitly admits 10 of the restricted cash as eligible, without making it usable for Monday obligations. Which result changes? Explain why a change in eligibility and a release of cash are not the same event.
4. A planned income bridge has a revised earnings figure of 46. Re-test both contract definitions. Treat exact eligibility as an assumption, not a conclusion you carry across two documents.

## 3. Say which valuation assumption drives the change

A continuing operating company has expected nominal unlevered cash of 26, 29 and 34 thousand dollars at the ends of the next three years. The continuing forecast is 2% perpetual growth and includes the corresponding reinvestment. The case's blended rate is 9%, sustainable by assumption. At the valuation date a prioritized third-party debt claim is worth 90 and independently verified distributable nonoperating cash is 16. No other claims or outside assets are present. The equity consists of one class with 20,000 shares.

1. Derive the year-four cash and year-three continuing value. Build operating value and equity value. Report value per share with its units.
2. Hold the rate fixed but change perpetual growth to 3%. Explain why the resulting change cannot be a new valuation you call verified until you check reinvestment mechanics.
3. Return to the initial case. Suppose 8 of the 16 cash is needed by the business and already represented economically in the operating value; treat it as excluded from the bridge's excess cash. Recompute equity and the price per share without recalculating unchanged operating value.
4. Diagnose this proposed “check”: use the initial enterprise value divided by shares, then compare it with the higher-growth per-share equity result and select the larger. Identify two distinct inconsistencies, not just an arithmetic error.

## Decision review standard

Pass a response only if someone else can reconstruct the key cash balance and value comparison. Score each dimension separately rather than rewarding an elegant final recommendation:

- **Date and basis:** labels time zero, future receipts, units and the cash or claim being valued.
- **Reconstruction:** uses a dated ledger or explicit valuation bridge and a genuinely independent check, such as re-adding cash movements to the ending balance.
- **Feasibility and value:** distinguishes the cash, contract or resource screen from the project's calculated worth.
- **Decision and evidence:** says what is approved under the scenario, what is only indicated, and which observation would reopen the call.

For self review, use 0 for absent, 1 for partial, and 2 for reconstructible in each dimension. A full score is not evidence that the modeled assumptions apply to a real firm. If a requested fact is missing, record a method to obtain it instead of letting a tool invent it.
